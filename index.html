Investigations

Real cases from my work, with identifying details removed.

Microsoft 365 account takeover

Situation. A fraudulent email went out from a real employee’s mailbox and passed SPF, DKIM and DMARC.

What I did. Read the headers, pivoted to Entra ID sign-in logs, then to the device and Purview audit logs. Found device code phishing, a rogue registered device and token persistence that survived a password reset. Revoked sessions, removed the device, and searched the tenant by attacker IP for other affected accounts.

Takeaway. A password reset isn’t containment, and passing email authentication doesn’t mean a message is safe.

Firewall brute-force attack

Situation. A recurring daily network outage.

What I did. Reviewed FortiGate authentication logs and found 5–10 login attempts per second from several countries against an internet-exposed HTTPS management port. Confirmed the exposure on Shodan, checked the IPs on AbuseIPDB, and closed the port.

Takeaway. Management interfaces don’t belong on the internet.

FortiGateShodanAbuseIPDB

UDP flood and firewall CPU

Situation. A FortiGate 80F running at high CPU.

What I did. Traced the bottleneck in the anomaly logs to a UDP flood, applied a hardware-accelerated DoS policy and cross-referenced the malicious IPs. CPU load dropped from 85% to 15%.

Takeaway. Read the anomaly logs before changing anything.
