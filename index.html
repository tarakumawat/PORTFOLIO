Investigations

Real cases from my environment, with sensitive identifying details removed.

Microsoft 365 Account Takeover

MITRE: T1566.002, T1528

Situation: A fraudulent email went out from a real employee’s mailbox and successfully passed SPF, DKIM, and DMARC checks.

What I did: Read email headers, pivoted to Entra ID sign-in logs, and then to the endpoint and Purview audit logs. Uncovered device code phishing, a rogue registered device, and token persistence that survived a password reset. Revoked active user sessions, removed the rogue device, and queried the tenant by attacker IP for other impacted accounts.

Takeaway: A password reset is not complete containment on its own, and passing email authentication mechanisms does not automatically mean a message is safe.

Firewall Brute-Force Attack

MITRE: T1110

Situation: Investigated recurring daily network slowdowns and resource usage anomalies.

What I did: Reviewed FortiGate authentication logs and identified 5–10 login attempts per second originating from multi-country IPs targeting an internet-exposed HTTPS management port. Confirmed the exposure status on Shodan, checked source reputations on AbuseIPDB, and closed public exposure of the port.

Takeaway: Administrative management interfaces should never be exposed directly to the public internet.

FortiGate Shodan AbuseIPDB

UDP Flood & Firewall CPU Exhaustion

MITRE: T1498

Situation: A FortiGate 80F experiencing severe CPU spikes impacting office throughput.

What I did: Traced resource bottlenecks in anomaly logs to a massive UDP flood. Deployed a hardware-accelerated DoS policy rule and cross-referenced malicious IPs against threat intelligence. Successfully reduced firewall CPU load from 85% down to 15%.

Takeaway: Always analyze anomaly and traffic logs thoroughly before modifying general policy configurations.
